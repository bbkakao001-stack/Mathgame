6.1 · ENERGY STORES

Store or source?

A store describes where energy is held. A source is the object or system from which energy is supplied in a situation.

StoreChemical energy is stored in food, fuels and batteries.
SourceA battery can be the source supplying energy to a lamp.
Describe the situationName the source first, then describe the stores and transfers involved.
6.1 · STORES

Movement, height and shape.

Objects can store energy in different ways. The situation tells you which store matters.

KineticAn object has kinetic energy when it is moving.
Gravitational potentialA raised object has energy associated with its position above the ground.
Elastic potentialA stretched or compressed elastic object stores energy.
6.1 · STORES

Chemical and thermal stores.

Two more stores help explain everyday changes.

ChemicalFood, fuels and batteries contain stored chemical energy.
ThermalThermal energy is associated with particles moving in a substance.
Use precise namesSay “chemical energy in the fuel” or “thermal energy in the water”.

A trolley starts moving.

When a person pushes a shopping trolley and the force makes it move, energy is transferred. Scientists describe this as work being done.

ResultEnergy is transferred and the trolley gains kinetic energy.

DID YOU KNOW? · TESLA

A slowing EV can send energy back to its battery.

Tesla calls this regenerative braking: when Model 3 slows with the accelerator released, the motor can generate electrical power and feed surplus power back to the battery.

Energy storyKinetic energy of the moving car → electrical energy → battery.
Why it mattersRecovering some energy during slowing can help increase driving range.
Real-world limitRegenerative braking can be reduced when the battery is cold or already fully charged.

6.1 · BOUNCING BALL

Each bounce is lower.

A bouncing ball transfers energy through several stores. The ball usually rebounds to a lower height after each impact.

FallGravitational potential energy transfers to kinetic energy.
ImpactThe ball deforms; energy transfers to elastic potential, thermal energy and sound.
ReboundSome energy returns to motion and height; some is dissipated to the surroundings.
6.2 · USEFUL AND WASTED

Useful depends on the task.

Useful energy is transferred in the way we want. Wasted energy is transferred in an unwanted way for that task.

Task: light a roomLight is useful; heating the surroundings is usually wasted.
Task: heat foodEnergy transferred to the food is useful; sound escaping from the oven is wasted.
Ask “useful for what?”The same type of energy can be useful in one task and unwanted in another.

6.2 · DISSIPATION

Energy spreads out.

When energy transfers to the surroundings and becomes more spread out, it is described as dissipated. It has not disappeared.

BeforeEnergy is concentrated in a store or transfer that can make a chosen change happen.
AfterSome energy spreads to the surroundings as heating, sound or vibration.
Less usefulThe energy is harder to use to make the original change happen again.
6.2 · CONSERVATION

Wasted does not mean gone.

Energy is not destroyed when it is dissipated. It is transferred to the surroundings and becomes less useful for the intended task.

Account for energyInput energy is transferred to useful outputs and less-useful outputs.
Check the languageSay “dissipated to the surroundings”, not “used up” or “vanished”.
6.2 · SANKEY DIAGRAMS

A Sankey diagram represents energy transfers. Wider arrows represent larger amounts of energy.

InputThe input arrow enters the device.
Useful outputThe useful transfer continues in the direction of the task.
Wasted outputA branch shows energy transferred in an unwanted way.

DID YOU KNOW? · SPACE

Spacecraft do not all use the same power source.

NASA explains that spacecraft may use sunlight, batteries, or radioisotope power depending on where they travel, what they must do and how long they must operate.

Near the SunSolar panels can convert sunlight into electricity.
During darknessBatteries can supply stored electrical energy.
Deep spaceSome missions use heat from radioactive decay when sunlight is too weak.

DID YOU KNOW? · NUCLEAR

A nuclear power station still uses steam and a generator.

In a nuclear plant, fission produces heat. That heat boils water, steam spins a turbine, and the turbine drives a generator to produce electricity.

Different heat sourceNuclear fission replaces burning fuel as the source of heat.
Same big ideaSteam transfers energy to a turbine.
GeneratorThe moving turbine drives a generator.

DID YOU KNOW? · DEEP SPACE

Voyager is powered by heat from plutonium-238 decay.

Each Voyager probe carries radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs) that convert heat from the radioactive decay of plutonium-238 into electricity.

Not a solar panelVoyager operates far from the Sun where sunlight is much weaker.
Heat firstRadioactive decay continuously produces heat.
Electricity nextThe RTG converts part of that heat into electrical power.
